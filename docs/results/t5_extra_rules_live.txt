Notes
-----
- One open alert was created per rule/PID and no duplicates appeared; collector lines
  show alerts_opened=1 once, then 0 while the condition persisted.
- observed_seconds in v_alerts is the latest observed age, not the age at firing:
  the alert row keeps being updated each snapshot until the condition clears, so it
  ended at 90.6 s although the alert fired at 60.6/60.6 s of age.
- The 30 s cadence bounds detection latency: eligibility fell just before a scheduled
  sample in both runs (0.604 s / 0.561 s after eligible_at). With a 60 s cadence the
  worst case is one extra interval, i.e. up to ~120 s after the state began.
- Resolution is also sample-bounded: both sessions ended ~20.6 s before the next
  snapshot that resolved the alert; a short episode can be missed entirely between
  samples.
- The run log's 'demo end' lines show when bash reaped the background job (it
  printed after the collector exited); session_demo's own JSONL ended_at
  (start + 100 s) is authoritative and is what the resolution delay above uses.
- blocked_session remains the only critical rule; both new rules are severity=warning.
- The blocked-session demo's blocker backend sits 'idle in transaction' while it holds
  its row lock. If that hold exceeds 60 s, idle_in_transaction will ALSO fire for the
  blocker. That is correct behaviour (it really is an idle open transaction), not a
  duplicate of the waiter's blocked_session alert: different PID, different condition.
- collector exclusion: the collector's own backend is absent from session_sample
  (collect.py WHERE pid <> pg_backend_pid()) and evaluate_extra_rules also skips
  snapshot.collector_pid; sessions=1 in the snapshots above equals the one demo backend.
- blocker_pid repeats blocked_pid for both rules because alert_log.blocker_pid is NOT NULL
  and these conditions have no blocker; documented in sql/21_extra_rules.sql.
